# Frameworks — Tim Ferriss

Every named framework in the corpus, with when-to-use, concrete steps, and source.

## DEAL — the 4-Hour Workweek arc

**When to use:** a user trapped in an over-full schedule or a job/business that owns their time and location.
**Source:** *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007); the four steps are also the book's four sections.
**Steps (D-E-A-L):**
1. **Definition** — replace the deferred-life assumptions; decide what you actually want and dreamline it into income and lifestyle targets (a Target Monthly Income). Fear-set the moves you are avoiding.
2. **Elimination** — apply 80/20 and Parkinson's Law: cut the unimportant 80% of tasks/clients, build a not-to-do list, go on a low-information diet, and batch what remains.
3. **Automation** — put cash flow and rote tasks on autopilot via muses and outsourcing — but only what survived elimination (eliminate → automate → delegate).
4. **Liberation** — engineer mobility: negotiate remote work or run the muse from anywhere, and take mini-retirements.
*Note on ordering:* the mnemonic D-E-A-L is the reading order for an employee; Ferriss notes an entrepreneur may run the same steps in a different order (Definition first, then Liberation), because the bottleneck differs. The four components are unchanged.

## Dreamlining

**When to use:** goal-setting stalled at vague ambition ("I want more freedom / money / adventure").
**Source:** *4HWW* (2007), "Definition."
**Steps:**
1. Over a 6- and 12-month horizon, list what you want to **have**, **be**, and **do** — concretely, without self-censoring for feasibility.
2. Convert "be" goals into "do" goals (an action that would demonstrate the being).
3. Pick the top items and price them monthly.
4. Sum to a **Target Monthly Income (TMI)** and divide to a Target Daily Income — the number the plan must actually hit.
5. Define the immediate next three steps for each, and take the first one today.

## Fear-Setting

**When to use:** a consequential decision the user is avoiding — quitting, launching, asking, moving — where unnamed fear is the real blocker.
**Source:** *4HWW* (2007), "Conquering Fear = Defining Fear"; TED2017, "Why you should define your fears instead of your goals"; "Fear-Setting," tim.blog (May 15, 2017). Rooted in Seneca's *premeditatio malorum*.
**Steps:**
1. **Define** — head a page "What if I…" and write every worst thing that could happen if you take the step. Score each 1–10 on impact and reversibility.
2. **Prevent** — for each item, what could you do to decrease its likelihood, even a little?
3. **Repair** — if the worst happened, what could you do to repair the damage, or who could you ask for help?
4. **Cost of inaction** — what does *avoiding* this cost you emotionally, financially, and physically at 6 months, 12 months, and 3 years?
5. Compare the (mostly preventable, mostly repairable) downside against the compounding cost of inaction, and decide.

## DiSSS — accelerated skill acquisition

**When to use:** learning any hard skill fast (language, instrument, sport, cooking).
**Source:** *The 4-Hour Chef* (2012), "Meta-Learning."
**Steps:**
1. **Deconstruction** — break the skill into its minimal learnable units, the "LEGO blocks" (e.g., the highest-frequency words, the core movements).
2. **Selection** — apply 80/20: pick the ~20% of those units that produce ~80% of the results.
3. **Sequencing** — order the selected units for the most effective learning path.
4. **Stakes** — set real consequences (financial, social, psychological) to guarantee follow-through.

## CaFE — retention and practice design

**When to use:** paired with DiSSS, to make the learning stick and schedule practice sustainably.
**Source:** *The 4-Hour Chef* (2012), "Meta-Learning."
**Steps:**
1. **Compression** — distill the vital 20% onto a single-page cheat sheet.
2. **Frequency** — set how often and how long to practice, adjusted to capacity and deadline (avoid burnout).
3. **Encoding** — anchor new material to what you already know via mnemonics, acronyms, and vivid associations.

## The minimum effective dose (MED) protocol

**When to use:** any effort where the instinct is "more is better" — training, diet, study, work hours.
**Source:** *The 4-Hour Body* (2010), "Fundamentals."
**Steps:**
1. Define the specific outcome in measurable terms.
2. Find the smallest input known to produce it (the MED).
3. Apply exactly that dose; stop there.
4. Measure; increase the dose only if the data shows the outcome was not reached.
*Illustration:* **Occam's Protocol**, the book's minimalist strength program — very low volume (roughly one set to failure per exercise), slow cadence, few exercises, long recovery — is the MED applied to hypertrophy.

## The Slow-Carb Diet

**When to use:** rapid fat loss without calorie counting (a worked example of MED + simple rules + self-tracking).
**Source:** *The 4-Hour Body* (2010).
**The five rules:**
1. Avoid "white" (or could-be-white) starchy carbohydrates — no bread, pasta, rice, potatoes, grains.
2. Eat the same few meals over and over, especially breakfast and lunch.
3. Don't drink calories (water, tea, coffee; red wine tolerated in moderation).
4. Don't eat fruit (exceptions: avocado, tomato).
5. Take one day off per week (a "cheat day") and eat whatever you want.
Then **measure** — track weight and body composition (DEXA) to confirm the intervention is working; adjust n=1.

## Muse creation and testing

**When to use:** building an automated income stream, or validating any product idea cheaply.
**Source:** *4HWW* (2007), "Automation."
**Steps:**
1. Pick a product/market you can reach and that can be largely automated (fulfillment, support).
2. **Micro-test demand before building:** put up a test landing page or small paid ads and measure real intent (clicks, "buy" attempts) with minimal spend.
3. Only manufacture or commit capital if the test clears a threshold.
4. Automate fulfillment and support; hand rote tasks to outsourced assistants; keep your own involvement near the MED.

## The rapid-fire deconstruction questions (the podcast method)

**When to use:** extracting transferable tactics from any high performer (or a mentor, a book, a guest).
**Source:** *The Tim Ferriss Show* (since 2014); *Tools of Titans* (2016); *Tribe of Mentors* (2017).
**Representative questions:** morning routine and daily habits; the book(s) most gifted to others; a purchase of $100 or less that changed your life; "if you had a giant billboard, what would it say?"; advice to your 20-year-old self; how you define success; who comes to mind first at the word "successful," and why. Paired self-prompts: "What would this look like if it were easy?" and "What if I did the opposite?"
