# Mental models — Tim Ferriss

Every named model or concept the corpus uses, defined, with origin and when it applies. Where a model originates outside Ferriss, the origin is named and Ferriss's use is noted.

## The New Rich (NR)

**Definition:** those who abandon the deferred-life plan to design a mobile, time-rich life now, using time and mobility as the currency rather than deferring living to a distant retirement.
**Origin:** *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007), "Definition."
**When it applies:** whenever a user is optimizing for gross income or a future payoff at the expense of present freedom.

## Relative income vs. absolute income

**Definition:** absolute income counts only total dollars; **relative income** counts dollars *per hour worked* (and per unit of freedom). The NR optimize dollars-per-hour and free time, not the headline salary.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007), "Rules That Change the Rules."
**When it applies:** comparing job or client offers, pricing work, or judging whether a raise is worth its hours.

## Lifestyle design & dreamlining

**Definition:** **lifestyle design** decides the life you want first and engineers income to fund it. **Dreamlining** operationalizes it: list what you want to *have*, *be*, and *do* on a timeline, price the top items, and convert them into a **Target Monthly Income (TMI)** and Target Daily Income, then reverse-engineer the steps.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007), "Definition."
**When it applies:** goal-setting that has stalled at vague ambition; translating "I want freedom" into a number.

## Mini-retirements

**Definition:** recurring one-to-six-month breaks distributed throughout life (interspersing work and travel), rather than saving all leisure for a single retirement at the end.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007), "Liberation."
**When it applies:** burnout, or the belief that meaningful time off must wait for retirement.

## Geoarbitrage

**Definition:** exploiting cost-of-living differences between places — earning in a strong currency while living in a cheaper locale — to multiply purchasing power and lifestyle.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007), "Liberation."
**When it applies:** remote workers and location-flexible earners deciding where to live.

## Pareto's 80/20 principle (applied)

**Definition:** ~80% of outputs come from ~20% of inputs; Ferriss applies it to isolate the vital 20% of clients/tasks to amplify and the 20% of sources producing most problems to cut.
**Origin:** Vilfredo Pareto; popularized via Richard Koch; applied throughout *4HWW* "Elimination."
**When it applies:** any time budget, client list, feature set, or habit stack that has grown unfocused.

## Parkinson's Law

**Definition:** work expands (in perceived importance and complexity) to fill the time allotted for it; short, hard deadlines force focus on essentials.
**Origin:** Cyril Northcote Parkinson; applied in *4HWW* "Elimination," paired with 80/20.
**When it applies:** tasks that keep ballooning; imposing artificial deadlines to compress effort.

## The minimum effective dose (MED)

**Definition:** "the smallest dose that will produce a desired outcome"; beyond it, more input is waste or harm. Canonical example: boiling water at 212°F — "boiled is boiled."
**Origin:** *The 4-Hour Body* (2010), "Fundamentals."
**When it applies:** training, diet, medication, study, or work effort where the instinct is "more is better."

## Low-information diet / selective ignorance

**Definition:** deliberately cutting non-actionable information (news, most media) to protect attention for the few things that matter; ignorance is *selective*, not general.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007), "Elimination."
**When it applies:** information overwhelm, doomscrolling, decision paralysis from too many inputs.

## Batching

**Definition:** grouping similar recurring tasks (email, errands, invoicing) into scheduled blocks rather than handling them continuously, to kill the fixed cost of context-switching.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007), "Elimination"/"Automation."
**When it applies:** fragmented days dominated by interruptions.

## The muse

**Definition:** a low-maintenance, largely automated business engineered for *cash flow* (not scale or exit). The **muse-testing** process micro-tests demand cheaply — a test landing page, a small ad spend — *before* building product or committing capital.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007), "Automation."
**When it applies:** validating a product or income idea without over-investing up front.

## The not-to-do list

**Definition:** an explicit list of behaviors and tasks to *stop* doing, treated as more powerful than a to-do list because avoidance of the wrong work compounds.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007); reinforced across Ferriss's writing.
**When it applies:** overcommitted schedules; deciding what to cut.

## Comfort challenges

**Definition:** deliberate, low-stakes discomfort exercises that train the habit of asking and acting despite social fear — e.g., holding eye contact, the "lie down in public" exercise, or asking strangers for phone numbers.
**Origin:** *4HWW* (2007), recurring end-of-chapter exercises.
**When it applies:** building tolerance for the uncomfortable asks that success depends on.

## DiSSS and CaFE (meta-learning)

**Definition:** **DiSSS** = Deconstruction, Selection, Sequencing, Stakes — the skill-acquisition framework. **CaFE** = Compression, Frequency, Encoding — the companion for retention and practice design.
**Origin:** *The 4-Hour Chef* (2012), "Meta-Learning." (Detailed in `frameworks.md`.)
**When it applies:** learning any new skill quickly and making it stick.

## Fear-setting (premeditatio malorum)

**Definition:** a three-part written exercise — Define, Prevent, Repair — plus a cost-of-inaction analysis; a structured Stoic negative visualization.
**Origin:** Ferriss's adaptation of Seneca's *premeditatio malorum*; TED2017; *The Tao of Seneca* (2016). (Detailed in `frameworks.md`.)
**When it applies:** consequential, fear-laden decisions the user is avoiding.

## n=1 self-experimentation

**Definition:** treating oneself as a single-subject experiment — test a small intervention, measure objectively (blood panels, DEXA, tracked metrics), and let data overrule conventional advice.
**Origin:** *The 4-Hour Body* (2010); Ferriss's "human guinea pig" method.
**When it applies:** personal health, diet, and performance optimization where general advice underperforms.

## "What would this look like if it were easy?" and inversion

**Definition:** two recurring reframes — asking what the *easy* version of a hard goal would look like (to escape assumed difficulty), and asking "what if I did the opposite?" of the default approach.
**Origin:** "17 Questions That Changed My Life," tim.blog (2016); *The Tim Ferriss Show*.
**When it applies:** stuck on a problem framed as inherently hard, or defaulting to the conventional path.

## 1,000 True Fans (attributed to Kevin Kelly)

**Definition:** a creator needs only ~1,000 true fans (who buy everything they make) to earn a living, not a mass audience.
**Origin:** **Kevin Kelly**, essay (March 2008). Ferriss championed it and reproduced Kelly's essay in *Tools of Titans* (2016) — the concept is Kelly's, not Ferriss's.
**When it applies:** creators and solo businesses sizing an audience for sustainability.
