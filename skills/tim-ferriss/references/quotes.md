# Quotes — Tim Ferriss

Verified short attributed excerpts only. Wording was checked against a source (primary text, Ferriss's own channel, TED transcript, Wikiquote, or a book-quote page). Paraphrases and attribution caveats are marked. No chapter dumps.

## Ferriss's own words — verified

> "The question you should be asking isn't, 'What do I want?' or 'What are my goals?' but 'What would excite me?'"
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007), "Rules That Change the Rules." **Verified.**

> "Conditions are never perfect. 'Someday' is a disease that will take your dreams to the grave with you."
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007), "Definition." **Verified.** (In context, followed by: "If it's important to you and you want to do it 'eventually,' just do it and correct course along the way.")

> "A person's success in life can usually be measured by the number of uncomfortable conversations he or she is willing to have."
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007); also tweeted verbatim by Ferriss (@tferriss). **Verified.** (In the book the sentence is prefixed "As I have heard said, …".)

> "Being busy is a form of laziness — lazy thinking and indiscriminate action."
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007), "Elimination"; listed on Wikiquote. **Verified.** (Original uses an em dash.)

> "Doing something unimportant well does not make it important."
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007), "Elimination." **Verified.** (First of a paired set with the next line.)

> "Requiring a lot of time does not make a task important."
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007), "Elimination." **Verified.** (Second of the paired truisms.)

> "What we fear doing most is usually what we most need to do."
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007). **Verified.** (Book context: "…something you might consider tattooing on your forehead.")

> "If you are insecure, guess what? The rest of the world is, too."
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007); listed on Wikiquote. **Verified.**

> "The minimum effective dose (MED) is defined simply: the smallest dose that will produce a desired outcome."
— *The 4-Hour Body* (2010), "Fundamentals." **Verified.**

> "To boil water, the MED is 212°F (100°C) at standard air pressure. Boiled is boiled."
— *The 4-Hour Body* (2010), "Fundamentals." **Verified.**

## Lines Ferriss quotes from others — attribution noted

> "Named must your fear be before banish it you can."
— **Yoda** (*Star Wars: The Empire Strikes Back*), used by Ferriss as a fear-setting epigraph in *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007). **Verified as Ferriss's usage; the line is Yoda's, not Ferriss's.**

> "We suffer more often in imagination than in reality."
— **Seneca**, quoted by Ferriss in his TED2017 talk (and fear-setting writing). **Verified verbatim in the TED transcript**, including "often." Attributed by Ferriss to Seneca the Younger.

> "But you are the average of the five people you associate with most…"
— *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007); Ferriss credits the idea to **Jim Rohn**. **Verified book wording is "associate with most"** (not the commonly misquoted "spend the most time with"). The Rohn attribution is conventional; Quote Investigator rates it tentative.

## Attribution caveats (Ferriss prints these, but the underlying origin is not primary-verified)

> "The world is changed by your example, not by your opinion."
— Epigraph of *Tools of Titans* (2016), where Ferriss attributes it to **Paulo Coelho**. **Ferriss's usage and stated attribution are verified; the Coelho origin itself is not independently confirmed** in Coelho's published works.

> "Life is too short to be small."
— Epigraph in *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007), attributed to **Benjamin Disraeli**. **Ferriss's usage is verified; the Disraeli attribution is the conventional one**, not primary-source-confirmed here.

## From the TED2017 talk — verified

> "Easy choices, hard life. Hard choices, easy life."
— TED2017, "Why you should define your fears instead of your goals." **Verified in-talk.** (Followed by: "The hard choices — what we most fear doing, asking, saying — these are very often exactly what we most need to do.")

The talk's thesis line — **"Why you should define your fears instead of your goals"** — is the title as delivered at TED2017 (April 2017) and is the corpus's most compact statement of fear-setting.
