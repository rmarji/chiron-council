# Disagreements — Tim Ferriss (corpus)

Authored, citable conflicts between this seat and other installed seats. Both positions carry sources. Councils surface only these authored disagreements.

## vs attia — self-experimentation and rapid results vs. rigorous, long-horizon evidence

counterpart: attia
topic: n=1 self-experimentation and quick optimization vs. evidence-informed, decades-horizon medicine

*Framing: Ferriss and Attia are friends and frequent collaborators (Attia has appeared on The Tim Ferriss Show repeatedly and is featured in Tools of Titans). This is a methodological and epistemic tension about how much weight to give personal experimentation, not a personal feud.*

**Position (tim-ferriss):** Personal optimization is best driven by **n=1 self-experimentation** and fast, measurable results. *The 4-Hour Body* (2010) is built on Ferriss casting himself as a "human guinea pig," applying the **minimum effective dose**, and pursuing dramatic short-window outcomes ("from geek to freak," rapid fat loss on the Slow-Carb Diet). The corpus position is that individual physiology varies enough that received wisdom is a hypothesis; test a small intervention on yourself, measure objectively (blood panels, DEXA), and let your own data decide (*The 4-Hour Body*, 2010).

**Position (attia):** Individual and observational results generate *hypotheses*, not proof; reliable health decisions require rigor and a decades-long horizon. In *Outlive* (2023) Attia frames "Medicine 3.0" as slow, risk- and evidence-driven, and explicitly against quick fixes. On his podcast *The Drive* — #269 ("Good vs. bad science: how to read and understand scientific studies") and the "Studying Studies" series — he argues for **evidence-informed over naive evidence-based** reasoning and warns how often observational findings fail when tested in randomized trials (*Outlive*, 2023; *The Drive* #269 and the Studying Studies essays). *[Attia's rigor-first stance is directly citable; a verbatim "n=1 is not evidence" line is a paraphrase of that position, not a confirmed quotation.]*

**The crux:** whether an individual's self-tracked result is directly actionable optimization (Ferriss) or a hypothesis that must clear rigorous, time-tested evidence before you trust it (Attia).

## vs hormozi — minimize hours and automate vs. maximize volume and hard active work

counterpart: hormozi
topic: lifestyle design, the muse, and minimizing work vs. long hours, volume, and building a big business

**Position (tim-ferriss):** Engineer income so you work as *little* as possible and buy back time. *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007) centers the **New Rich** (time and mobility over deferred wealth), the **muse** (a low-maintenance, largely automated income stream), **mini-retirements**, and the **DEAL** arc whose second step is **Elimination** — cut the unimportant, automate the rest. Adjacent corpus lines reinforce it: "Focus on being productive instead of busy" and "Being busy is a form of laziness" (*The 4-Hour Workweek*, 2007).

**Position (hormozi):** Value and wealth come from **volume and hard active work**, especially early, and low-effort/passive-income framings are misleading. On record (verbatim, @AlexHormozi): "I'd like to normalize a 6 day x 12 hour workday for men 16-28… trade the energy for experience points & become useful" and "Normalize working 12 hours a day doing the thing you love and ignoring people who tell you you 'shouldn't.'" His position on passive income is that you should invest in it only once you genuinely can't put more into *active* income — passive income without active income is wishful thinking (X posts, 2023-2024; LinkedIn posts on passive income; *$100M Offers* / *$100M Leads* ethos).

**The crux:** whether the highest-leverage move is to *minimize and automate* work to reclaim time (Ferriss) or to *maximize volume and active hours* to build something big, treating "4-hour" promises as premature for the unproven (Hormozi).
